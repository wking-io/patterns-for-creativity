# AI can do your job...now what?
This is the core loop of building products:
Research -> Creativity -> Manufacturing -> Feedback

I said products, not businesses. A business has a lot more moving pieces: distribution, finances, IT, operations, blah blah blah. But at the center of building a product, this loop shows up over and over again.

And traditionally you could bucket each of these into familiar roles

Research -> Product Managers
Creativity -> Design
Manufacturing -> Engineering
Feedback -> Support

However, this is VERY misleading.

When you go a layer deeper on all of these the loop still looks the same.

### Design
Research -> Discovery
Creativity -> Brainstorming
Manufacturing -> Prototypes
Feedback -> User Testing

### Engineering
Research -> Spikes
Creativity -> Prototypes
Manufacturing -> Production Code
Feedback -> Observability

And the other two are no different. This loop is ingrained in all of our work.

Here comes the important part. One of these things is not like the other.

What is the goal of Research? To find and organize as much information about "something" that you can.

What is the goal of manufacturing? To take a spec, a well defined outcome and make it a reality.

What is the goal of feedback? To collect and organize data on a system to identify good and bad behaviors.

What do alllllllll of these have in common? Before the work starts, you already know what the outcome is. The work to be done is very well defined.

and what is AI good at? Work with well defined outcomes.

So what should we be doing with all of this work? We should be handing off as much of that work as we can to AI workflows. To factories of our own creations. Why?

Because AI can do that part of your job. And you should let it! Why?

Because THIS is your new job. Creativity.

But before we go any further I want to make sure we are on the same page about something. Design does not own creativity. Creativity is not this [show something highly visual] creativity is this [show something with discovery]

It's the work required when you don't know the outcome yet, when the answer isn't obvious. Creativity is the process of exploring what is possible to figure that out.

Before work can get handed off to manufacturing. Before it can get handed off to whatever factory you are using.

You have to know what work is worth producing.

That is why we are talking about creativity.

Because that is where the leverage is. Where humans in the loop really matter.

So, how do you do it? How do you get better at creativity?

Well I think the first thing we should answer is what does creativity look like?

It isn't this [show illustration of a factory machine]. You don't know where the journey ends before you start it.

With creativity you start by just trying somethings. You hit a wall. You make something bad. You notice one small part of it that works. So, you pull that thread, but you end up abandoning that idea, and try a bunch of others. Later, you come back to the one you abandoned with better understanding. You borrow from somewhere unexpected. You keep shaping the thing and exploring dead ends until it the right solution feels inevitable.

[Make a growing illustration tracking this process]

This is what creativity looks like. It looks like madness, and someone else could be handed the same problem, and their path could look very different.

That doesn't mean there is no method to the madness.

There are patterns, there are tools, there are practices not to manufacture creativity, but to create the environment where discovering the right idea becomes inevitable.

Because at its core, creativity has a rhythm.

The rhythm of creativity is a cycle of expansion and contraction.

[<Expand<>contract>]

You ask every question and then decide which answers matters.

You explore countless ideas to shape the path worth taking.

Then you do it again, and again, and again. This is why creativity can look so chaotic.

It isn't about being gifted. It is about being relentless.

> The output of creativity is not the artifacts you make along the way. The output is the understanding you gained while making them.

Uncovering the right answer, not through a single moment of inspiration but because you found enough wrong answers to recognize the right one when it showed up.

Okay, enough yapping. let's get into the good stuff.

There are two areas that we're going to focus on to improve your creativity:

The first is capacity.

Your capacity is the ceiling on your creativity. This is an over simplification to make a point but it is your ability to come up with ideas.

And it is follows the same rhythm we already discussed.

- expansion: Broad Experiences
- contraction: Deep Expertise

Craft

Your Craft is how capable are you working in your medium. Putting pen to paper. Another over simplification but this is your ability to make ideas real.

Surprise! It's this again.

- expansion: exploration
- contraction: feedback

Let's start with your capacity. How can you raise your ceiling for creativity?

### Pattern: You can't have ideas for things you never notice
Most people let these moments pass them by. Something makes them feel frustrated, or they see something that makes them feel excited, and that is as far as it goes. They let that reaction roll over them and never think anything else about it.

You can't have ideas for things you never notice.

When you have a reaction to something, build a habit of answering why? Why do I like this, why do I hate this? How would I do it better? How did they do that? That tiny pause is where all creativity begins.

## Pattern: Optimize for exploration
You have to bring your ideas into contact with reality. You have to define those blurry edges. You have to uncover everything that was hidden. There is a very good chance when the idea you have perfected in your head makes contact with reality it falls apart, but even if it doesn't  regardless of your experience. Regardless of how well defined the problem or idea you are working on is there is one truth to the creative work:

- Your first idea always sucks

You might end up choosing your first idea, but it still sucks.

Why?

We talked about this already. What is the goal of creativity?

> The output of creativity is not the artifacts you make along the way. The output is the understanding you gained while making them.

The goal is to uncovering the right answer, not through a single moment of inspiration but because you found enough wrong answers to recognize the right one when it showed up.

So yeah, your first idea sucks. Because you haven't seen enough to know it doesn't.

So how can you optimize for exploration? You make starting less sacred.

Getting started no matter how experienced you are can be a tough hurdle. Sometimes you have a blank canvas either literally or metaphorically, but the more common trap is having one idea that you can seem to move beyond.

Okay we are masters of exploration now. But there is a rhythm. We have expanded, you have to contract. The goal of exploration is not exploration. It is getting the right signal. Getting to the right answer, and so we need to cover the last pattern.

## Pattern: Shorten the loop
This is where a lot of people get tripped up with creative work. They finally get started, but then they try to solve the whole world. All at one time.

It is overwhelming. It is too much to solve at once. So either they give up, or they get lost. They spend too much time in the woods and never get to a solution.

You have to work at the right fidelity. What I mean by that is that the artifacts that you are producing need to be efficient. They need to focused. You should be asking yourself "what is the one thing I am trying to learn?" and "what is the smallest thing I can make to learn it?"

Because the goal isn't perfection it is clarity.
